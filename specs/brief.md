# Brief

Owner: Mark Semenov (CEO). Built only from `research/interviews/2026-10-12-mark-vision.md`; every line cites the record entries it comes from. The earlier brief is kept as background in `research/brief-2026-08-dmitry-synthesis.md`.

## Where we are going

Dream Islands sells measurable results from travel, not room nights: guests and partners both measure a trip by its outcome (A1, A1 follow-up).
By late 2029 it is a recognised brand: guests track and share their progress, partners are certified and display the Dream Islands plaque, and Dream Islands is present in each partner's infrastructure (A1).
The path runs in three phases: 70+ guests/month and ~$1M a year no later than 12 months — the milestone for sustainable profit — then ~700 guests/month and ~$10.9M at 36 months, and $100M revenue at 60–72 months (A8, E1).
Focus is Asian destinations, with guests coming from all over the world (A8).
The next 6 months are about B2B: more than 10 partners paying for the system, then white-label and API distribution (A9).

## Problem

- Partners cannot show their wellness advantages (air, silence, bedding, food, spa, programs) or sell wellness packages through ordinary OTAs (A4).
- Partners have no view of how their practitioners and programs perform (A1, A4).
- Outside high-end luxury, properties give the guest nothing beyond a booking confirmation; the guest has to chase them for information (B7).
- Buying a wellness trip takes many questions: today it runs through WhatsApp for two weeks to a month per guest (B7).
- Onboarding a partner is manual: PDFs, pictures, websites and WhatsApp are read and re-entered by Mark; it takes about a month to go live (B1, C10).
- Payments run on WhatsApp invoices to a Thai account, mostly via Wise; Stripe is barely used (A5).
- An unformalised commercial model with the pilot partner led to different readings of the 15% commission base, direct payments to the partner and periodic cash gaps (B13).
- Partners that work on paper must be chased by email and Line before every arrival (B3, B9).
- What happens to the guest after the trip is not tracked today (B11).
- Most processes still depend on Mark personally (B7, B16).

## Who it is for

**Guest**
- Residents of large cities with above-average income and heavy mental and physical load: top managers and entrepreneurs who look after their health (A2).
- Example: the first guest (2024), 59, a top manager at Singapore television, bought a VIP detox package, 8 days / 7 nights, about $3,000 (~90,000 THB), for longevity, prevention and curiosity (A2).
- Guests mostly come with a concrete aim: weight, sleep, stress, exhaustion, overload, skin and endocrine issues; budget is often the deciding factor (B6).

**Partner**
- Properties with wellness infrastructure (hotel or resort, medical/wellness centre, pool of practitioners) and their sales, revenue, marketing and operations managers (A1, A4 follow-up).
- Example: Village Hotel Sentosa, Singapore (A1).
- What they get: wellness-first positioning, new packages built with Dream Islands, analytics on practitioners and programs, distribution through white-label and API (A4).
- What they watch: ADR, length of stay and add-on revenue from Dream Islands channels (A1).

**Practitioners and facilitators** use the platform to report to their managers, log guest interactions and see their own performance (B3).

**Not served**: the classic all-inclusive mass market and people not looking for health or self-development; Dream Islands does not target people for whom it would not solve the problem (A2).

## What it must do

Guest side
- R1. Let the guest take a self-serve assessment (physical, mental, social) and receive a dashboard with risks and growth points by email (B6).
- R2. Recommend programs through Dream Islands' own ranking model across cohorts (A7, B6).
- R3. Connect the guest's health trackers (Apple Health, Whoop and others) with the guest's permission (A1, B6, B12).
- R4. Take the guest's travel context (alone, with family) and location into the match (B6).
- R5. Show the guest progress as graphs and let them share it to social networks and messengers (A1).
- R6. Hand a conversation from the AI agent to a person whenever it involves medicine, documents or a tailor-made request (B7).
- R7. Follow up automatically with guests who go silent (B7).
- R8. Before arrival: the guest fills in their own data; send a pre-arrival guide a month and a week before (B9).
- R9. During the trip: a daily push with schedule, goal and highlights; collect tracker data (B10).
- R10. After the trip: recommendations, progress against the guest's baseline, and paid online sessions or content (B11).
- R11. Propose the next trip when a signal changes (assessment, tracker, behaviour); a concierge takes over after the guest replies (B12).
- R12. Give 3,000 THB off the next visit for every referred guest who travels (A3).

Partner side
- R13. Let a partner apply with a short form that creates a CRM record and first profile without a person (B1).
- R14. Pre-qualify partners with a scoring agent; an onboarding/account manager makes the final approval; medical services get a separate review (B1, B2).
- R15. Collect wellness attributes as selectable tags, each confirmed by documents, certificates or photos (B2).
- R16. Review certification once a year, using real bookings and guest ratings; give awards to the best (B2).
- R17. Build programs from reusable inventory to the Dream Islands standard (goal, guest profile, baseline, activities, practitioners, duration, inclusions, measurements, expected progress) (B1, B15).
- R18. Have the partner enter and confirm most of the data; Dream Islands does not re-type PDFs (B1, C10).
- R19. Treat a partner as live only once a real guest can pass end-to-end (B1).
- R20. Take room-night availability and dynamic rates from the partner's channel manager, or from Excel for partners without one; no room, no package (B3, B4, C6).
- R21. Let practitioners and their managers update their data, rate classes and give feedback on guests (B3, B10).
- R22. Attach the full schedule at booking and show it to the partner and every practitioner involved (B4).
- R23. Partner cabinet V1: Product Builder, Distribution Manager, Program Intelligence (B15).
- R24. Show partners their SEO, GEO and LLM visibility with Dream Islands (B1).

Booking and money
- R25. Confirm bookings instantly unless the partner is on stop-sale or the contract says otherwise (C6).
- R26. Take payment online under the booking regulation: 10% deposit, balance 7 days before arrival, 10% off for full prepayment 90+ days ahead (B8).
- R27. Show prices in the guest's currency by location; pay each partner in the currency of its inventory (B8, C4).
- R28. Apply Dream Islands' cancellation policy on top of the partner's (B8).
- R29. A missed class is not refunded to the guest; the practitioner is not paid if it is cancelled 24 hours ahead (B4).
- R30. Fix machine-readable commercial rules for each partner before the first sale; no rules, no bookable inventory (B13).
- R31. Record every payment, including direct payments to the partner and extras, against one booking with a snapshot of its terms (B13).
- R32. Set the partner payout rule per contract; send a monthly partner statement and reconcile (B13, C3).

Growth and control
- R33. Know for each guest the source, contact details, ICP, cohort and on-site behaviour (B5).
- R34. Distribute inventory through white-label and API, and open it to an agent network at the same time (A6).
- R35. Report to the founder and investors from one data model (B14).
- R36. Let AI act automatically only inside set rules; some decisions AI proposes and a person approves; some are human-only (B16).
- R37. Before confirming a guest with a serious medical condition, review the case with the partner's medical lead (A10).

## Non-goals

- Not a travel marketplace or Booking.com for retreats (A11).
- Not a luxury travel agency or concierge (A11).
- Not a clinic or telemedicine platform; Dream Islands is not a licensed medical practice (A10, A11).
- Not a PMS, and not a full channel manager competing with the large ones; basic channel-manager functions for our own channel and its distribution are in scope (A11, C7, E6).
- Not a generic AI travel agent, and not a marketing agency for wellness properties (A11).
- Not basic travel requests such as finding a villa or assembling an ordinary trip (A11).
- No diagnosis, prescription, treatment changes or promised medical outcomes (A10).

## Constraints

- Clinical decisions stay between the guest and a licensed specialist; a serious medical problem is referred to medical care (A10).
- Guest data: minimum necessary and explicit permission; booking access is not health-data access; personal health data is never sold; aggregate analysis is de-identified (A10).
- A partner is paused at once on a credible risk to guest health or safety, unlicensed medical practice, a serious privacy breach or misleading claims; repeated quality problems lead to removal even if GMV is good (A10).
- Consent, privacy and clinical-escalation rules must be formalised with legal and regulatory advice for each country (A10, C9).
- The ranking model and the destination → object → program → specialist data stay in-house and separate from third-party platforms (A7, C1).
- Operations, connectivity, money, bookings and partner settlement run in Business Central; this is decided (C1, E7).
- On site, the property is responsible for the guest; Dream Islands supports and escalates (B10).
- Cura stays until its contract ends (about 6 months); the move in-house is decided (B7, C7).
- Payments come into a Thai account today, and through the Singapore entity later (C3).
- Paying cash on site stays as an exception for the pilot partner only; elsewhere it could carry a surcharge of about 7% (B8).
- If money or time halves: cut marketing and headcount; white-label and API stay (A9).
- AI never moves money, admits or removes partners, widens access to guest data or creates legal commitments on its own (B16).

## Success criteria

- In 6 months: more than 10 partners paying for the system (A9).
- In 6–12 months: white-label and API live, with the agent network (A6).
- No later than 12 months (October 2027): 70+ guests a month — the milestone for sustainable profit — about $1M GMV, 100+ standardised programs, repeat-journey rate measurable and heading for 20–30%+; growth continues from there (A12, E1).
- Red flags at 12 months: fewer than 30 guests a month, no growth, repeat under 10%, partners using Dream Islands only as a lead channel (A12).
- In 12–18 months: about 50 properties, at least 30 paying about $75 a month (A8).
- At 36 months: 700 guests a month, average booking $1,300, about $10.9M, about 1,000 programs (A8).
- 500 paying properties as the B2B milestone: optimistic case by 36 months, moderate case by 60–72 months (A8, E2).
- At 60–72 months: $100M revenue, 6,500 guests a month (A8).
- A new partner goes live within 10 working days, ideally about a week, and gets its first booking within a month of going live (B1, E8).
- The three numbers for investors: GMV, repeat-journey rate, paying properties (B14).

## Open questions

- Team size and number of countries per phase are not set and must be tied to clear calculations (A8, E3); A1 pictured about 100 people and a large office by 2029.
- Bookkeeping currency (SGD or other, possibly multi-currency) is open; investor reporting is in USD (C4, E4).
- Payout moment: after the free-cancellation window or after checkout; left per contract for now (C3).
- Commission versus subscription (15% with a subscription, 20–25% without) is a hypothesis (B2).
- Monthly prices are hypotheses: partner subscription $50–150, guest premium $10–30, online sessions $50–150 an hour (A5).
- 30/60/90-day follow-up with trackers is important (B11, E5); how it shows up in reporting is still to settle (B14).
- Who sees health data: minimum necessary (A10) versus the property's account manager and Dream Islands staff seeing it (C8).
- What stays with Cura until the contract ends, and how much work each in-house agent stage is (C7).
- The Dream Islands Wellness Standard, marketplace liquidity criteria, unit economics and failure/recovery workflows are not yet defined (B17).
